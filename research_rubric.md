# SCALE Research Prioritization Rubric

*How we decide which studies advance. Applies to all SCALE research, whatever the funder, partner or product.*

## The five criteria

Rate each criterion **Low / Medium / High**. Each criterion is weighed against another, so the trade-offs are part of the rubric.

### 1. Impact on the field
**Question:** How many decisions does this study move, and how directly?

| Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|
| The finding matters to one partner or product. No clear decision depends on it. | The finding is relevant to one group of decision-makers or to a narrow context. It connects loosely to other portfolio work. | The finding moves current decisions for several audiences (practitioners, developers, policymakers, funders). It fills a named gap in a cluster (a set of studies that triangulate the same underlying construct). It is framed at the level of mechanism, so it stays useful after any one product or tool changes. |

**Trade-off:** A study can score high here without asking the most novel question. Complementing existing portfolio work counts.

### 2. Level of uncertainty
**Question:** Is the answer still unknown, or is it already settled?

| Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|
| Strong evidence already exists for this population and context. We would mostly be replicating it. | Some evidence exists, but it is correlational, from a different population, or mixed. | There is little or no causal evidence for this population or context. A null result would change investment as much as a positive one. |

**Trade-off:** New questions often lack established measures, which pulls against rigor. Where possible, we pair a new question with a validated proxy.

### 3. Speed of insight
**Question:** How soon does a finding that can inform a decision arrive?

| Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing usable arrives until the final confirmatory result, more than 18 months out. | Some interim findings arrive, but most of the value comes at the end. | The design is rapid-cycle. Descriptive or formative findings arrive before confirmatory results. Short-term outcomes are validated proxies. |

**Trade-off:** Speed can compete with rigor, because short-term proxies are weaker than durable outcomes. We reduce this by using proxies that have been validated against longer-run measures. We don't invent new ones.

### 4. Sample size
**Question:** Is the sample large enough to detect the effect we care about?

| Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|
| The study is underpowered for the primary effect. | The study is powered for the primary effect but not for key subgroups. | The study has adequate power for the primary effect and for key subgroup moderators. |

**Trade-off:** Larger samples cost more and take longer to recruit. We weigh that against speed, and against whether the question needs that level of precision yet.

### 5. Rigor
**Question:** Does the design support the causal claim we want to make?

| Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|
| There is no credible comparison group. The analysis is not pre-specified. | The comparison group is quasi-experimental, or the analysis plan is only partly pre-specified. | The comparison condition is strong. Randomization is feasible. The study is pre-registered. |

**Trade-off:** Stronger designs, such as randomization, can be harder to negotiate with partners and slower to launch. We weigh that against speed, and against whether a descriptive or quasi-experimental answer is enough for the decision at hand.

> *The Low/Medium/High anchors (including the 18-month mark) are starting points. Adjust them to fit how the team actually works.*

---

## How we use it

**This is a holistic check, not a weighted score.** We don't add the ratings into a single number. A study advances when:

1. **It clears a reasonable bar on most criteria.** In practice, that means Medium or better on at least three of the five.
2. **Strength elsewhere offsets any weakness, and we can say how.** For example, a descriptive study with lower rigor can be justified by speed if it serves as a pilot for a later RCT.
3. **It has a stated place in the portfolio.** We can name the cluster or construct it contributes to and the gap it fills.

**Red flags that warrant a second look:**
- The study is Low on both Impact and Uncertainty. That usually means it answers a question nobody needs answered.
- The study is High on Uncertainty but Low on Rigor, with no plan to validate its measures.
- The study is High on Speed only because its proxies have never been validated.
- No one can say which cluster the study belongs to.
